| D-06 | **Automation gate.** The brief's BR-06 and architecture principle 3 describe a gate that opens and closes by itself, but its scope table puts automatic switching in Advanced and manual on/off with shadow mode in the MVP. | D-08 | **Who admits an invoice to a batch.** BR-17 and the AI actor row say the AI puts an invoice into a batch, but "What AI does not do" and BR-27 say the AI does not decide. Decision: the Rule Engine admits an invoice; the AI supplies evidence. | D-09 | **Cross-entity duplicates.** The cross-entity detection text (Advanced) mentions an invoice "sent to another entity", but duplicate detection is MVP. Decision: in the MVP an exact key duplicate (supplier, series, number) is checked across the whole group, without revealing the other entity's data (BR-09). | D-10 | **Mid-period batches.** The process table allows signing mid-period, but the scope table puts it in Advanced. | D-11 | **Bank step.** The process table lists the Bank paying, but real bank payment is out of scope. Decision: the signed batch goes to the simulated ERP, which returns a paid status. There is no Bank actor in the MVP. | Process step 5 is simulated. |
| D-12 | **Demo Operator.** The brief has ERP simulator screens for creating purchase orders and confirming receipt but names no actor, and BR-02 and BR-13 need different people for each role. Decision: add a demo-only actor, Demo Operator, who plays the buyer, warehouse keeper, and acceptance personas. It belongs to the simulator, not to the product. | Fills a gap. |
| D-13 | **Shared-service model.** The competitor section claims a shared-service model, but every finance actor is scoped to one entity. See R-16. |
| D-14 | **Goal definitions.** Employee-expense invoices are excluded from the G-01 denominator because they are separated, not processed (BR-23). ## Corrections to the brief from research

These come from the research done in this step. Sources and dates are in [Regulatory context](10-regulatory-context.md), [Similar products](04-similar-products.md), and [Goals and metrics](02-goals-and-metrics.md). All pages were read on 2026-10-31.

| ID | Correction | What the brief says | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-15 | **The 2025 e-invoice sentence is removed.** | "From 01/06/2025 businesses with revenue from VND 1 billion must use tax-authority-coded e-invoices", citing a Sapo blog. | The Government portal summary of Decree 70/2025 ties the VND 1 billion threshold to household and individual businesses, and for enterprises the test is direct sale to consumers. It covers cash-register e-invoices, not purchase invoices between businesses. The Sapo page does state the broader sentence, but it is a vendor blog and the Government portal does not support it. Decree 70/2025 was itself replaced from 2026-07-01 (D-17), and an EY summary says Decree 254/2026 no longer obliges consumer-facing businesses to adopt cash-register invoices. |
| D-16 | **The "why now" rationale is replaced.** | "Right time: from 01/06/2025 ... coded e-invoices", so no OCR is needed. | New rationale in [Context and problem](01-context-and-problem.md): (1) e-invoices have been mandatory between businesses since 2022, so structured XML input already exists; (2) since 2026-07-01 a coded e-invoice is the general rule under Decree 254/2026; (3) a purchase of VND 5 million or more keeps its VAT deduction only if it is paid by non-cash means (Decree 181/2025, Art 26), so the payment step now carries a tax consequence; (4) the list of risky sellers is large (more than 6,042 on 2026-09-22 in one vendor's compilation). The brief's point that line-item names stay free text still holds. The documents do not claim that 2026 "made" invoices structured. |
| D-17 | **The legal basis is updated.** | The constraints row cites Decree 123/2020 as amended by Decree 70/2025. | From 2026-07-01 Decree 254/2026 on electronic invoices and documents replaces Decree 123/2020 (as amended by Decree 70/2025), and Circular 91/2026/TT-BTC replaces Circular 32/2025/TT-BTC. The brief is dated 2026-10-31 yet cites the old base. The replacement is confirmed by one secondary source (an EY summary); a Sapo summary confirms the dates of Circular 91 but not what it replaces. The official texts were not opened. |
| D-18 | **Authority name is "Tax Department"; the XML format version is treated as configuration.** | "General Department of Taxation"; XML format Decision 1450/QD-TCT as amended by 1510/QD-TCT is the item to confirm. | The General Department of Taxation was renamed the Tax Department, under the Ministry of Finance, on 2025-03-01. Decision 1450/QD-TCT is dated 2021-10-07 and is amended by 1510/QD-TCT. A newer decision on the same subject, 1271/QD-CT of 2025-05-30, exists. Whether it replaces 1450 and 1510 is **Unverified**, and Decree 254/2026 leaves invoice formats to the Minister of Finance. So the documents do not name a single XML version: the XML adapter reads the schema version from each file and the supported versions are configuration (O-09). |
| D-19 | **The 05-TT claim is reworded.** | The consolidated request "follows the 05-TT paper form, which lists many payables with invoice numbers in one request", resting on Circular 200/2014. | Circular 99/2025/TT-BTC replaced Circular 200/2014 from 2026-01-01. One source says Circular 99 still lists form 05-TT; another page (dated 2026-06-18) still cites Circular 200. The page the brief cites does not say that 05-TT itself lists many invoices; it describes a separate detail form for that and advises one request per expense group. The consolidated request per supplier is therefore **this platform's own design**, configured per entity (BR-20). The batch-signing justification does not depend on form 05-TT. It depends on the Accounting Law rule that a voucher carries the signature and full name of the preparer and the approver. |
| D-20 | **The benchmark references are replaced.** | Touchless: market average about one third, best group about 49%. Cycle time: best group 3.1 days, average 10.9 days (Corpay). | The one-third and 49.2% figures are reported by two vendor pages citing a paid Ardent Partners report that could not be read; a third source (Hackett Group, via Corpay) reports 60%. The Corpay page the brief cites does not contain 3.1 or 10.9 days. Ardent's own January 2026 post gives 8.2 days as the 2025 average. G-01 shows a range and G-05 uses the Ardent figure (see [Goals and metrics](02-goals-and-metrics.md)). |
| D-21 | **The tax-risk list is a simulated list with a source and date on each entry.** | "The list of high-risk businesses has passed 6,042 businesses" (MISA AMIS). | The page says "more than 6,042" as of 2026-09-22 and is MISA AMIS's own compilation from several sources, not one official list. The simulated list carries a source and a date for every entry so that a real list can replace it. |
| D-22 | **Retention basis is stated.** | "Keep invoice XML files for 10 years as required." | Accounting Law Art 41(5)(b) sets at least 10 years for accounting vouchers used directly to post the books and prepare financial statements. The MVP keeps invoice XML for 10 years as a design choice, and, as the brief says, only designs the retention policy without implementing it. |
| D-23 | **G-05 is measured at the 95th percentile.** | "AI processes one invoice in at most 1 minute", with no percentile. | The smallest sensible choice: the target applies to the 95th percentile of the time from invoice received to the Rule Engine decision. The median is also reported. |

## Decisions from review of Step 1 (D-24..D-26)

These decisions were made during document review of Step 1, not by the user. They answer open items O-11, O-12, and O-13, they are applied in the Step 2 documents, and they are awaiting user review like the decisions above.

| ID | Decision | Effect on the brief | Closes |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-24 | **Signed non-coded XML is a trust level of its own.** The existing levels stay; one level is added, "Signed non-coded XML". It applies only to sellers in sectors listed in configuration, electricity being one (L-03). The seller's digital signature confirms where the invoice came from. A matching recurring profile is the independent source (BR-17, BR-24). If no profile matches, the invoice goes to an exception. For a **fixed price** the amount must equal the contract. For a **usage-based price** the profile gives only a plausibility check through the BR-24 deviation threshold; it is **not** an independent confirmation of the amount. This limit is stated plainly in BR-17 and is a call for the user to review (O-16). | BR-17 gains this case, together with the rewording of D-08. | O-11 |
| D-25 | Removed (D-33). **BR-32, non-cash payment.** A signed batch is a non-cash transfer instruction by design. UC-26 records the payment method and the payment document reference that the Entity ERP returns with the paid status. When a recorded payment is cash, at or above the configured VAT threshold (VND 5 million including VAT, summing purchases from one seller on the same day, L-12), the invoice is flagged with a warning that its VAT deduction is at risk, and the flag is written to the audit log. The platform does not block the payment, because payment happens outside the platform. D-33 removed this from the MVP: BR-32 is now Advanced (W-21), and a signed batch stays a non-cash transfer instruction by design. | Adds BR-32. The brief has no such rule. | O-12 (no longer applicable, D-33) |
| D-26 | **BR-01 keys on the buyer tax code.** A tax-code mismatch is rejected, and the correction follows the BR-31 path (D-07). A buyer-name mismatch with a matching tax code is a warning only. This rests on one secondary summary of Circular 91/2026 (L-07); the official text was not opened. | BR-01 says "tax code does not match the ordering entity", as the brief does, and now also says that a name difference alone is not a rejection. | O-13 |

**D-33 and this table.** D-25 was a call made in review and was never in the brief. The user then decided to remove the cash payment flag (BR-32, DF-26) from the MVP together with foreign invoices (see [Step 2 decisions, D-33](../02-usecases-userstories/00-decisions.md#scope-change-decided-by-the-user)). The non-cash rule stays as regulatory background (L-12) and in the "why now" rationale (D-16).

Kept as proposed in the review, and awaiting user review like the rest: D-23, the decision not to create ledger entries (O-06, A-11), the Advanced item numbers W-01..W-18 (W-19..W-21 were added by D-33), the ID families F-xx and L-xx, the module-to-use-case mapping, the G-01 and G-07 denominators, the fictional Calderwood names, and the "NOT PLANNED" header of the future-work page.
